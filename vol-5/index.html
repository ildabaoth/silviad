Volume V of The Silviad

The Son

begun 920

Kept by Laurentios. In this volume: Laurentios, and then Philostrate.

Opened at Regensburg in the seventh month of 920, where Volume IV closed: the ledger stays in the hand of Laurentios, the Greek soldier who wrote the last nine days of the king's life, and it now follows a son who is not as great as his father, knows it, and is determined not to let the dynasty die. It is the volume of the partition, of a crown that needs two men to put it on, of a kingdom that is vast on the map and not one country, and of four names that did not swear.

Part I

The Son's Crown

920 – 921
1

The Son's Crown

920 – 921 · in the hand of Laurentios

On the twenty-eighth day of the sixth month of 920 the King of Bavaria dies and his son, eighteen and marked with the smallpox, inherits a holy war, twenty-nine vassals who owe him nothing and eighty-eight counties of which the crown holds four. Bavarian custom takes two thirds of the royal lands in an afternoon and gives them to six boys. The war is fought twice and won twice — Mainz outnumbered nearly two to one, and Sayn with the largest army this realm ever raised and not one dead man in the record. Then a coronation that takes a season: a duke and a pontifex who will not look at each other, eight hundred people in ecstasy in a square for six words in a chancery, five men who lead the oaths and four who do not swear, a crown nailed to the kingdom so that custom cannot have it, and an oath of conquest sworn by a man who already holds everything. In the twelfth month he marries a woman the record calls a pillager, and the winter is the quietest in this history.

Part II

The Detractors

921 – 921
2

The Detractors

921 – 921 · in the hand of Laurentios

In the same week of January 921 the peasants of Bavaria rise under Svetozár and the Saxons rise under Willichin for a church and for Bohemia, and neither of them is the rebellion the last chapter was braced for. The holy war grinds on at Neuss, at Sayn twice, at Veldenz, with not one dead man in the record. In the middle of it the court goes to a six-month tournament in Hungary, where an unknown challenger called Gáspár shoots three arrows into the mark after a man is carried off the field, and the King of Bavaria wins the archery and does something foolish to liven the crowd. Then the reckoning: four unsworn lords taken alive on the day the Saxon rising is beaten and not killed, Xenophon dead at a siege, Euthykrates dead in another at thirteen with a victory and a defeat behind him, his counties back in the crown's hand, Aquileia given away to a man with a Norse name seven days later, and the King on the road for Rome on the twenty-fifth, with his ledger-keeper going with him and refusing to say on the page whether it was understanding or flight.

Part III

The School at Rome

921 – 922
3

The School at Rome

921 – 922 · in the hand of Laurentios

Six days after his brother is buried the King of Bavaria leaves Regensburg for Rome and takes a hundred and six days over the Alps to arrive on the eighth day of the second month of 922, where the see that kept his father at the gate in 908 puts him on a roll of students instead. He defeats Abbot Baldassarre II soundly in disputation, impresses a master called Landolfo twice, writes a poem about a cat hunting mice, learns the ceremonies of a city he had already seen at six, and watches his own confessor fail his examinations four times without buying him out of it. At home, the peasant war ends in one line, a daughter called Sophe is born on his twentieth birthday, two of his brothers pass under dukes, and on the first day of the tenth month three corps lose five regiments each with no battle fought.

Part IV

Rome Again

922 – 924
4

Rome Again

922 – 924 · in the hand of Laurentios

The King comes home from his first Roman school to a realm of eighty-eight counties, a crown with four of its own, and an army fifteen regiments smaller than when he left. In the third month his father's oldest enemy, Pilgrim of the house of the Aribonen, is killed in a duel by a man the record does not name; nineteen days later the third Chalcedonian rising begins in Bavaria under Dietrich, for Bohemia, as Heilwich's did in 908 and Willichin's in 921. It is beaten on the fifth day of the fifth month, the fourth war for Hesse is declared the day after, and twenty-one days after that the King leaves for Rome on foot with a party of pilgrims, Germano, and Stoyanka of Silistra. In Rome the last scholar of the company dies, the King weeps at a church door, translates a monument for his fellow pilgrims as his father did at Tunis, and hears that Dusica has borne him a second daughter. He comes home in February a Mystic and a Prolific Pilgrim, and no longer a Wise Man, and eighteen days later the peasant leader of 921 dies in his cell.

Part V

Hesse and the Wedding at Modruš

924 – 925
5

Hesse and the Wedding at Modruš

924 – 925 · in the hand of Laurentios

The fourth war for Hesse is fought at last: Diez on the first day of the fourth month of 924 with 466 of ours dead to 335 of theirs, and Siegen in the eighth month with 265 to 273, and then on the second day of the tenth month Fulda and Kassel go to Euxenippos, Marburg and Nassau come in under Friedrica and Udo, and a realm of eighty-eight counties becomes ninety-three. Six days after Siegen the King leaves for a wedding in Croatia that runs eight months, in a castle where a countess calls her husband a cur in public and then falls in love with him at first sight, a chaplain lambasts a peacock and chokes on a goose bone, a guest is reprimanded for drunkenness and another makes a scene about the unchaste mingling, and the King slips out of a banquet with a count and comes back with a friend. In the fourth month Pontifex Prijezda dies, and Austria passes to Vasilka; in the seventh month the chancery calls a king of twenty-two August.

Part VI

Croatia

925 – 927
6

Croatia

925 – 927 · in the hand of Laurentios

Three months after the wedding at Modruš ends, the King declares war on Croatia against Sulvan and it runs two years. Bihać on the first day of 926 is won at two to one, and on the same day the record first calls him an Aggressive Attacker; Zagreb in the second month is the cheapest battle in the book at ninety-eight dead; and on the first day of the twelfth month at Križevci one thousand nine hundred and eighteen of his men meet one thousand seven hundred and sixty-six and nine hundred and ninety-nine of his are killed, the worst day in the record. Three months later another army, outnumbered by a thousand, wins at Senj. In the middle of the war there is a five-month tournament at Vasvár given by Count Bohodar, whom he imprisoned in 921, where he kills a peasant by accident on the butts on the twenty-second day of the fourth month of 927 and wins the archery on the fifteenth of the fifth. The war ends with Gacka and Lika kept, Modruš in under its wedding host, ninety-six counties, and two Moravian counties given to a brother of ten.

Part VII

Bosnia and the Hunt

927 – 929
7

Bosnia and the Hunt

927 – 929 · in the hand of Laurentios, and then Philostrate

Three weeks after Jihlava and Znojmo go to a boy of ten, Emiliya dies of illness, the second woman of the old company in twelve months, and the record gives her nothing else. On the tenth day of the eleventh month of 927 the King declares war on Lower Bosnia against Pribunja, a boy born in the twelfth month of 917 who is not yet ten. The King is ill from the twelfth month through the second of 928 and the war goes on without him: Bihać in the snow on the thirtieth day of the first month of 928, 1,406 against 429, fifty-six of ours and two hundred and ninety of his; then five months with no battle in the record; then Kuclat on the twenty-third day of the sixth month, 1,347 against 188, fourteen of ours and all of theirs. Seventy of ours and four hundred and seventy-eight of theirs for one county. On the first day of the tenth month the King goes out to a hunt that runs four months, and on the twentieth day of the tenth month Laurentios signs his last page in the room off the yard and dies on the same day. Philostrate, sixteen, the King's sister, takes the pen and finishes the year: a stag hunt led by Euktemon and scolded for wildflowers; Pribunja imprisoned and Krajina to Thearidas, a man not in the ledger; Polydoros insulting the King at his own Christmas court and nothing being done about it; Iancu dead, two Iancus in the record and no join; and on the first day of the second month of 929 a King of twenty-six comes home a Hunter with ninety-seven counties.
