Volume III of The Silviad

The Kingdom

906 – 908

Kept by Chrysogonos, and then Matako. In this volume: Chrysogonos, and then Matako.

Opened at Regensburg on the tenth day of the fourth month of 906, with a herald in a horse-market and a declaration that carries the Captain's own name in the claim for the first time in thirty-five years. The Greek writes the war, as he said he would, and puts the pen down at the crown; from the first day of the sixth month of 907 the book is the smith's, and the word in it is King. This volume holds the invasion of Bavaria, the field at Lengenfeld, the crown, and the year and a half that follows it: a coronation at Regensburg, a country whose church does not want him, and a road to Rome that he last walked as a student in 893.

The Crown

906 – 907 · in the hand of Chrysogonos, and then Matako

Fourteen months in which a company of hired swords declares a war in its captain's own name, destroys an army twice its size in a river, wins a battle against one man, rides four hundred miles mid-invasion to fight a tournament, and takes a king prisoner on a field it has used before. Pribunja, who was his ally in 888, comes out for his son-in-law's crown and does not come back. On the first day of the sixth month of 907 the record changes one word, and a third of the army goes home.

The King

907 – 908 · in the hand of Matako

What a crown is, measured in iron and paper. Five and a half months of coronation at Regensburg, three good omens in one winter, the old company being made into dukes and pontiffs, and a brawl in a hall. A king's son is born in a capital rather than a camp, the country's own faith rises against a Greek who was baptized in a Danube chapel for reasons of contract, and Hohenburg is won without numbers. Then eight people and forty mules on the road to Rome, a boy of six learning his letters on a mule, and the record stopping in a borrowed room in the Veneto.
