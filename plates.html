Plates

The House, and the North

The plates are not evidence. They are what the translator imagines while reading, and should be looked at the way one looks at the pictures in a manuscript — as memory, not record.

The eleventh month

The sea, and the shore after it

The events of the first chapter, before it is translated.

January 867 — May 871

The long detour

The events of the second chapter, in the order the ledger gives them. Two of these — the wall at Naniwa and the board at Hakata — are attested by a line each and nothing more, which is why the translator has drawn them so plainly.

871 — 874

The road west

The events of the third chapter, in order: a noble's errand, twelve soldiers, the richest plain in the world, and the first of the company to be left behind. The last three were drawn from the campaign's own likeness of Tokifusa, so that the man who takes up the book is the man the game made.

874 — 879

The City

The events of the fourth chapter, in order: a spear made for the house, a fight in the ravines, a death that needed nothing done, a cup from the old kings, a flower in a ledger, and the wall. The first plate of the seven is the chapter's frontispiece and was drawn from the campaign's own likeness of Edouinos at nineteen, aged forward ten years.

879 — 880

The irons

The events of the fifth chapter, in order: a forge under the wall, a killing and its sentence, the City on the inside, a tournament lost gladly, four hundred people on a Roman road, a small bronze god, and a prayer in a weed field. The first plate is the chapter's frontispiece, and the only portrait in the book of the company's smith, who wrote the end of it.

880 — 881

The muster

The events of the sixth chapter, in order: a rank read off a roll in a wet field, a forge and an anvil coming over a river, an armoury with a prisoner keeping its accounts, a deer that took six weeks, a night at a fire that the plate deliberately does not describe, two women and a file on a sandbank, and a battle the company won by nine men.

881 — 883

The statue of Hades

The events of the seventh chapter, in order: a cart in a flooded river, an empty church at an hour when nobody goes into a church, a wheel set in snow above Florence, the carts in the mud below a wall the size of a town, forty people in a vegetable market and a boy with a fish head, a faceless god bought in a cellar, a gate at Severin gone through in column and nothing thrown, and the last count of the volume under the house's standard.
