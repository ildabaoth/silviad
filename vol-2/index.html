Volume II of The Silviad

The Hired Sword

883 – 906

Kept by Chrysogonos, with Matako. In this volume: Chrysogonos, Matako, and then Raina.

Opened at Constantinople in the third month of 883, where Volume I closed: the log changes hands with the trade, and the trade changes from keeping things to changing them. Chrysogonos of the company writes it — a Roman Greek, a soldier since he was fifteen, who counts frontages and fees where Matako counted what the iron cost. Matako keeps pages of her own in every chapter, and they are marked as hers. This volume is about a company becoming an army, and about the first time a throne is saved by one. It passed Rome in the sixth month of 893 with its captain at a school desk and a lord of Severin for a friend, and it is not finished. It closes at Regensburg on the ninth day of the fourth month of 906, in a city on the Danube with two thousand men in it and no contract running, on the evening before the man who has no land declares a war for a kingdom of his own.

The Hills and the Duke

883 – 884
1

The Duke's Throne

883 – 884 · in the hand of Chrysogonos, with Matako

The company turns off the pilgrim road and into the Macedonian hills, then hires itself to Duke Tepes to save a throne: a wound nobody explains, a battle at Câmpulung in which an army of fourteen hundred is destroyed for the loss of seven men, and a wolf that a captain stares down alone in the last week of October.

The Lists

885 – 886
2

The Arithmetic of a Name

885 – 886 · in the hand of Chrysogonos, with Matako

Nine hundred miles east to a tournament on the Volga and a second place that paid better than first; four wars taken at once and one of them won; a board game won in front of the Emperor against a count who tried to cheat him out of it; a stag hunt with four villages paid to stay out of the woods and a beast that was never there; and an iron chain struck off in the eleventh month of 886.

Part III

Vidin

887 – 889
3

Vidin

887 – 889 · in the hand of Chrysogonos, with Matako

A Bulgar count with a parchment and no army comes to the camp in the rain, and the company goes to war for a claim: four armies destroyed in two years, the duke it saved in 884 fought and taken at Vidin, an emperor's revolt crushed at Athens, and a title changing hands on the sixteenth day of the fourth month of 889 — in a stone room where nobody says the company's name.

Part IV

The Board and the Kings

889 – 890
4

The Board and the Kings

889 – 890 · in the hand of Chrysogonos, with Matako

Nine hundred miles into the German lands for a board game, and the final is played against Matako; two counties that the company won pass to other men; his ward is examined and offered a place; Gavril raises a revolt against King Vladimir-Rasate and the company breaks a king's army at Brăila and another at Ploiești — and both kings are still on their thrones when the count of 890 is taken.

Part V

The Prisoners

891 – 892
5

The Prisoners

891 – 892 · in the hand of Chrysogonos, with Matako

Feodosia's army destroyed in the snow and the county held; a tournament at Galați where a duke and the man he took a county from sit at the same table; the archery won by waiting for the wind; Feodosia taken and imprisoned on the eleventh day of the seventh month; and on the twenty-seventh Hippostrate is murdered and Ana is put in irons, and the record names no killer. Wiesbaden, Nannuka's death at fourth hand, two revolts lost — and a captain asking where the law is written down.

Part VI

The Falls and the School

892 – 893
6

The Falls and the School

892 – 893 · in the hand of Chrysogonos, with Matako

The company takes Visegrad off the client it made; a third board-game title at Radom; and two men fall dead within three days of each other in a Polish field, and the record gives no cause. South through the Macedonian country to Rome, where the Captain of nineteen hundred men takes a room and enters a school at forty-three, learns badly and completely, and on the twelfth day of the sixth month of 893 sits in a garden with Tarasiy of Severin — eleven years after the two afternoons that started it. Volume II closes.

Part VII

The Liberty

893 – 898
7

The Liberty War

893 – 898 · in the hand of Matako, with Chrysogonos

The company declares a war of its own and wins it, and buries the men who came out of the north in 879 while it does. A prisoner dies in irons in Rome and the record gives the word for her and no cause; a book of theology is written in six weeks and bound in boards cut by the smith; Adelina of Muntenia keeps her country and loses her power; eight months of voyages with no reason in the record; and a man of forty-seven puts three champions on their backs at Naumburg. This part stops on the twenty-fifth day of the fourth month of 898, with the war still running and no land — and the volume, and the book, go on.

Part VIII

The Wife

898 – 901
8

The Wife

898 – 901 · in the hand of Matako, with Raina

The hired sword at his best and most restless. A contract ends in Muntenia and another is taken on two rivers in the east against three to one; the roll of 879 begins to come apart, and Tokifusa dies at sixty-five. Constantinople, which should have been home, lets the company in and watches it. He wins a duel at Tarnów at fifty with his best friend five months dead, is ill for seventeen months out of twenty, and on the sixteenth day of the first month of 901 marries a woman of twenty-eight who is not in love with him yet — and who writes a page of this book to say so.

Part IX

The Sons

901 – 906
9

The Sons

901 – 906 · in the hand of Chrysogonos, with Matako

Five years in which the man with no country acquires one without noticing. A son is born at Krassó and five weeks later he falls in love with his wife; a war is lost in the mountains, and the count of the fields gets shorter and bloodier, until Verona, where every one of two thousand four hundred is killed and six of ours. Ana is murdered in his own camp and nobody is punished. He walks out of the list at Győr and never says why. Three of the 879 roll die, and the company reaches Regensburg for the first time on the first day of the twelfth month of 904.
