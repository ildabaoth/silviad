The Arms of the House

Purpure, a sun in splendour of sixteen rays Or.

The device of the house of Silviades is a sun of sixteen rays, gold upon purple, and it carries no lettering of any kind. That last point is worth stating, because a reader who has met a dozen merchant marks will expect initials, and there are none: the arms belong to the house and not to the man who happens, at a given date, to be its head. Edouinos Silviades did not put his name on them and could not have taken it off.

The sixteen rays are drawn bladed rather than straight, and they are always an even number, so that one ray points up the middle of the shield. The gold is the gold of the device and the purple is the purple of the device; a copy in other colours is a copy and not the arms.

What follows is a list of the forms the device takes, and of what each form is for. They are not alternatives to one another. A house that carried a shield and nothing else would have nothing to fly, and a house that stamped letters with a shield would be wasting the one shape a seal wants, which is a circle.

On flying another man's arms

The company flies the arms of the house, unfringed, and it is not the house. This is the ordinary arrangement between a company and the family that raised it, and it holds until somebody with a better claim to the device, and a longer purse, decides that it does not.
